## Interpretation of operative processes

One of our key research areas is the interpretation of operative processes for intraoperative decision support in laparoscopic and robot-assisted surgery based on real-world video data. We develop computer vision and vision-language methods that recognize anatomical structures and surgical phases, predict patient outcomes from operative video, and train models across institutions without centralizing patient data. Some of the resulting clinically applicable tools are currently undergoing clinical evaluation.

## Prediction of disease trajectories and perioperative risks

Surgical procedures are inherently connected with the risk of complications. Early - ideally preoperative - anticipation of complications, their timely identification and treatment can considerably reduce their clinical impact. We use both statistical and computational methods for complication risk and outcome stratification in patients undergoing surgery, integrating preoperative imaging and clinical routine data, and explore methods to improve early detection and accurate management of complications. Many of our works in this field relate to outcome improvement in pancreatic surgery.

## Trustworthy and translatable AI

The benefit of AI-enabled tools for patients and caregivers depends on sound validation and reporting, security and safety, and well-conceived clinical validation and translation strategies. We contribute to shaping these standards together with the scientific community in our field by sharing our experiences and perspectives through original studies, reviews, comments and perspective articles.

## Open data

Progress in surgical AI depends on diverse, representative data to develop and benchmark models. At present, data scarcity remains one of the critical roadblocks in our field. Our lab has led multicenter studies and large-scale data collection efforts across institutions and countries, and we are strong advocates of open data as a foundation for collaborative, reproducible research in surgery. We welcome inquiries from teams who are interested in collaborations and data contributions to future releases.
